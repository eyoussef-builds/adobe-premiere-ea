# Requested course-video production polish

Choose a visual direction from the user's brief and available footage. No private brand, fixed palette, client example or historical approval is bundled. Cuts-only tasks do not use this workflow.

## Design and pacing

- Keep the speaker recognizable and the real room consistent when requested. Use concise cards/callouts over footage and authentic full-screen demonstrations where helpful.
- Give close/wide cuts a clear reason: emphasis, a new thought or a demonstration. Visible changes may work better than constant tiny pushes; recheck headroom, hands, microphone and source resolution. An entrance can be an eased fade or cut instead of a rapid face zoom.
- Show spoken questions as readable callouts, then let the answer return naturally to the speaker. Use concrete progressive diagrams for concepts; avoid dense lists read verbatim.
- Verify claims and project mappings from the current brief. Past client fees are not promises of viewer income. Do not invent receipts or demonstrations.
- Arabic requires proper shaping and RTL layout, with Latin product/file names kept LTR. A suitable licensed Arabic font and a shaping-capable renderer are necessary. Render at sufficient resolution and inspect joins, punctuation, digits and line breaks at final size. Font appearance is an editorial choice, not a universal preset.

## Browser demonstrations

Capture the actual authorized page with supported browser tools. Exclude private controls, personal data, browser extensions and unrelated windows. Respect any restrictions on expanded modules or private content; a logged-in session does not authorize editing the site.

Live capture and animated screenshots are different methods. For live capture, record actual interactions and disclose capture fps versus delivery fps. For screenshot scrolls, capture a bounded tall region, reveal intersection-loaded content by scrolling first, then animate an eased viewport with reading holds. Do not label it a live interaction or fabricate missing page content.

## Color, audio and revisions

Avoid unintended RGB/BGR or full/limited-range conversions. Where appropriate preserve source YUV and convert only new art to the correct color interpretation. Tag input and output consistently. Compare a fixed unzoomed dark ROI with the approved base to detect shifts; do not add cleanup merely to hide a render color error.

Preserve voice gain/timing unless audio work is requested. When copying AAC packets, preserve encoder priming and verify timestamps; a seek at zero can discard a priming packet. For sound design use licensed/original effects on a separate stem, timed to semantic events. Mix quietly enough for intelligibility, verify headroom and listen around cues. Prior-task approval does not authorize new sound work.

Keep earlier versions. Re-render only changed regions when safe codec/keyframe boundaries and compatible timestamps are proven; otherwise render normally. Arbitrary packet concatenation can corrupt playback or AV sync.

## Editable handoff and verification

Use native Premiere text/Motion/effects when required. A pre-rendered V2 overlay over matching base footage is another valid handoff, but its graphics are baked into pixels. State that clearly and retain source art, timings and rebuild settings. Do not call it fully native editable graphics.

Check the saved project against the current edit, actual output frame/timestamp counts, full decode, voice alignment, every visual event, Arabic text, browser content and CTA. Automated measurements and contact sheets do not prove subjective production quality or full real-time playback review. Deliver truthful checks and any limitations.
